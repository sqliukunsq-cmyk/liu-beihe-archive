# Synthetic Witnessing and Visual Evidence

**LIU BEIHE / 刘北河 — research framework, 2026**

## Synthetic Witnessing

A synthetic image can perform the social role of a witness without having witnessed an event. Generated and reconstructed images may participate in memory, testimony, and belief while lacking a direct camera-event relation.

Synthetic Witnessing names this condition. The framework asks what an image can testify to when no prior photographed event guarantees its evidentiary force.

## Visual Evidence

Photography and cinema have never been identical with truth. Yet photographic appearance has historically carried an important assumption: a camera was present before a scene. Generative moving image weakens that assumption. The image can now arrive before evidence.

Visual Evidence therefore asks:

- What remains of evidentiary force when no scene preceded the image?
- Can provenance become part of montage rather than external metadata?
- How do viewers complete narratives when date, place, witness, and archive are absent?
- How should institutions distinguish aesthetic credibility from documentary evidence?

## Practice method

Generative technology is used not as a stylistic filter but as a way to produce and test visual credibility. Construction, obstruction, damage, and absence remain visible so that an image can approach evidence while exposing its own construction.

## Related work

*Memory Without an Event* (2026) is documented as a generative photographic work: an image of an event with no witness, date, or place. It must not be described as documentary evidence or proof of a physical installation unless such an installation is later produced and documented.
