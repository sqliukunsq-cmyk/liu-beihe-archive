Authorship
After Generation

In generative moving-image practice, authorship is not exhausted by the prompt or by the production of an image. It continues through judgment, selection, refusal, sequencing, contextualisation and responsibility for what is finally allowed to circulate.

From production to judgment

When a system can produce many plausible images quickly, abundance changes the location of artistic labour. The decisive act may occur after generation: deciding which image is necessary, which is merely fluent, which repeats a model’s default visual hierarchy, and which should disappear. Selection is therefore not a neutral administrative step. It can become part of the form of the work.

The prompt is a condition, not a complete account

A prompt establishes conditions for generation, but it does not by itself describe the full chain of artistic decisions. In LIU BEIHE’s practice, the relevant chain can include research, prompt construction, repeated generation, deletion, image comparison, temporal editing, sound, sequencing, contextual framing and decisions about disclosure. This approach treats authorship as distributed across a process while keeping the artist accountable for the final arrangement and claims.

Refusal as a positive operation

Under generative abundance, refusal can be productive. Removing an image because it is too predictable, too polished, too culturally compressed or too eager to resolve ambiguity is not simply a failure to use available material. It is a way of shaping attention. This connects authorship to Negative Craft: making through deletion, limitation, delay and non-selection.

Authorship and provenance

Generated images complicate older shortcuts between photographic appearance and a prior photographed event. Authorship therefore also involves provenance: what process produced the image, what transformations followed, what claims accompany it, and how viewers are invited to understand its relation to an event, memory or archive. Provenance does not automatically establish truth, but it makes the production history available for scrutiny.

A working position

The question is not whether a machine or a person can be named as the single source of every visible feature. The more useful artistic question is where judgment enters the process, where responsibility is accepted, and how the final work distinguishes generation from decision. For LIU BEIHE, authorship after generation is located in this accountable structure of choices.
