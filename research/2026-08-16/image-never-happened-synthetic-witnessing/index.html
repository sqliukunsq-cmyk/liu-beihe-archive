The Image That Never Happened: Cinema, Provenance, and the Ethics of Synthetic Witnessing

The Image That Never Happened: Cinema, Provenance, and the Ethics of Synthetic Witnessing

Author: LIU BEIHE / 刘北河 Independent Moving-Image Artist & Filmmaker LIU BEIHE Research Archive / 刘北河研究档案

I am less interested in whether an image is fake than in what kind of claim it makes

The central problem of generative cinema is not that images can lie. Cinema has always staged, edited, reenacted, composited, and manipulated. What has changed is the industrial availability of a photographic-looking image that may have no prior scene at all. I call the obligation created by this resemblance the debt of the scene: the more a synthetic image borrows the visual authority historically accumulated by photography, the more carefully I must decide what claim I allow it to make. C2PA 2.4, released in April 2026, expands Content Credentials with mechanisms including crJSON and repository receipts. [1] This matters because provenance is becoming infrastructure rather than optional metadata. Yet provenance cannot decide meaning for us. A perfectly documented image may still be deceptive; a declared fiction may be ethically rigorous. The technical question—what happened to this asset?—and the epistemic question—what should I believe because of it?—must remain distinct. The U.S. Copyright Office's AI reports produce a parallel pressure on authorship. Its analysis of copyrightability emphasizes human expressive contribution rather than treating prompting alone as an automatic substitute for authorship. [2] The 2026 WGA agreement similarly moves the politics of AI toward licensing and accountability by requiring notice when writers' work is licensed to train commercial generative systems. [3] SAG-AFTRA's 2026 agreement strengthens protections around digital replicas and synthetics. [4] These developments change my studio practice. I no longer think a disclosure card at the end of a film is enough. I want a production method in which provenance, consent, selection and refusal exist upstream. I keep model and version information, major transformations, source notes, rejected generations and reasons for rejection. The public artwork should not become a compliance document, but the studio should be able to explain how the image came into being. Nam June Paik's work remains important to me because he did not treat electronic media as a transparent channel. In TV Cello, body, instrument, circuitry and screen become one unstable object; in Chinese Memory, television fragments, books, autobiography and geopolitics form a mediated memory rather than a transparent record. [5] [6] The lesson is not stylistic. It is methodological: the apparatus belongs inside the meaning of the work. The strongest objection to my argument is that photography never guaranteed truth. Staging, propaganda, cropping and montage predate generative AI. I agree. My claim is narrower: generative systems change the scale, cost and ontological relation between photographic appearance and prior event. They industrialize what I call synthetic witnessing—the experience of encountering an image that performs the grammar of testimony without necessarily having witnessed anything. In Alien Friend, I want to turn this problem into mise-en-scène. A room will recur with tiny inconsistencies: a door shifts, an object disappears, the direction of light becomes impossible. Sound, however, will remain continuous. The spectator first trusts the scene, then discovers that continuity was an inference. In Does AI Know It Is AI?, I want identity drift and spatial failure to become evidence of a system attempting to stabilize a world. My prediction for the next decade is not that synthetic images will destroy truth. It is that visual trust will migrate away from the isolated image toward chains of context, provenance, institutions and accountable authorship. The artist's task will not be to restore an impossible age of innocent images. It will be to design forms in which uncertainty can be perceived without becoming indifference. I do not want the viewer to believe every image. I want the viewer to become more precise about why an image deserves belief.
