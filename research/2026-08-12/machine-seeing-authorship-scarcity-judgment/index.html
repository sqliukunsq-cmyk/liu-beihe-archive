Machine Seeing, Authorship, and the Scarcity of Judgment

Machine Seeing, Authorship, and the Scarcity of Judgment

Essay

Generative AI is usually discussed as an explosion of productive capacity. Images that once required a crew, a location and days of post-production can now be produced in minutes. But abundance changes value. When visual production becomes cheap, the scarce resource moves elsewhere. I call this shift the Scarcity of Judgment. The history of image-making repeatedly linked skill to production difficulty. To make a convincing image, an artist had to control materials, optics, software or a large production apparatus. Generative systems partially separate visual sophistication from execution cost. The ability to produce an impressive surface is therefore becoming less distinctive. What remains scarce is the ability to decide what should exist. Why this image rather than the thousand alternatives? Why this duration, this face, this absence, this cut? Why should the viewer spend time with it? These are not technical questions. They are decisions of authorship. Recent copyright analysis in the United States is revealing in this respect. The Copyright Office continues to emphasize human expressive determination, arrangement and modification rather than treating prompting alone as sufficient authorship. The legal framework and the artistic problem are not identical, but both point toward the same pressure: human contribution becomes most legible where there is selection, structure and responsibility. Machine seeing intensifies this problem because AI systems do not merely execute human instructions. Models inherit statistical patterns from large datasets and bring their own defaults of composition, representation and probability. A creator who accepts the first plausible output is therefore not simply “using a neutral tool”; they are allowing the model’s prior visual culture to make decisions on their behalf. Authorship in the generative era is consequently less about claiming control over every pixel and more about maintaining control over the logic of the work. This includes rejecting default aesthetics, documenting choices, constructing relations between images, and taking responsibility for cultural representation. The more images machines can produce, the less artistic value can be located in production alone. Judgment, refusal and structure become central. Generative abundance does not end authorship. It makes weak authorship easier to expose.

中文摘要

生成式AI让高质量视觉生产变得廉价，但因此真正稀缺的资源转移到了判断：选择、删除、组织、节奏、责任与立场。刘北河将这种变化称为“审美稀缺 / Scarcity of Judgment”。
