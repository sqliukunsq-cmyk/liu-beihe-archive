# Cultural Compression

**LIU BEIHE / 刘北河 — research framework, 2026**

Cultural Compression describes the process through which culturally specific forms, symbols, histories, and visual conventions are reduced into statistically reusable shorthand inside generative systems.

The term does not describe cultural exchange. It describes a tension between recognizability and situated knowledge: a system may become increasingly capable of reproducing the appearance of cultural specificity while understanding less of the historical, social, and material relations that produced it.

## Research questions

- What is lost when cultural difference becomes a reusable visual token?
- How does machine recognizability differ from cultural understanding?
- Can generative resemblance increase while historical continuity decreases?
- How can moving-image practice make compression itself visible rather than merely reproducing its outputs?

## Distinctions

Recognition is not understanding. Visual availability is not historical continuity. Generative resemblance is not cultural memory.
